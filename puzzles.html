Retrieval and reasoning

Puzzles

Five optional, purposeful challenges for class starters, fast finishers and revision. They do not replace section evidence or formal assessment.

1. Safety decision chainSequence and justify

Challenge: Put these decisions in a defensible order: identify the hazard; stop unsafe work; protect people; tell the teacher; follow the approved response; record or review what happened.

Check the reasoning standard

A strong chain controls immediate risk first, follows teacher and school procedures, and uses review to prevent repetition.

2. Nutrient function matchConnect evidence

Challenge: Choose a familiar food and connect one nutrient to a body function, then name one limit on what that evidence proves.

Check the reasoning standard

Accurate answers link a nutrient to a supported function without claiming one food cures, detoxes or guarantees health.

3. Label detectiveCompare fairly

Challenge: Select two packaged foods. Match serving size, per-100-g values, ingredients order and relevant claims to the comparison question each can answer.

Check the reasoning standard

Use the same basis for comparison. Per serve answers a realistic-use question; per 100 g can compare unlike pack sizes.

4. Influence matrixClassify and reason

Challenge: Sort six food-choice influences into personal, social, cultural, economic, environmental or digital categories. Then show two interactions.

Check the reasoning standard

Influences overlap. A thoughtful answer explains interaction instead of blaming one person or one advertisement.

5. Guidance-to-design challengeApply and evaluate

Challenge: Use Australian guidance, a consumer context and one practical constraint to improve a ready-to-eat meal concept. State the evidence and trade-off.

Check the reasoning standard

The strongest response treats HelloEats as a design context only and waits for the teacher-approved task notice before claiming formal assessment completion.
